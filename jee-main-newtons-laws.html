Q1. A 0.5 kg block is pressed against the inner wall of a vertical rotating drum of radius 4 m. If the minimum angular speed to prevent it from sliding is 5 rad s⁻¹, find the coefficient of friction (g = 10 m s⁻²).

Answer: A. 0.1

Normal reaction supplies centripetal force: N = mω²r. At limiting equilibrium μN = mg, hence μ = g/(ω²r) = 10/(25×4) = 0.1.

Q2. Masses 2 kg and 1 kg hang on opposite sides of a light string over a smooth fixed pulley. Released from rest, find the distance travelled by their centre of mass in 2 s (g = 10 m s⁻²).

Answer: C. 2.22 m

Atwood acceleration = (2−1)g/(2+1) = 10/3 m s⁻². Each mass moves s = ½at² = 20/3 m in opposite directions. The COM displacement magnitude is |2s−s|/3 = 20/9 ≈ 2.22 m.

Q3. A block takes 50% longer to slide down a rough 45° incline than down an identical smooth incline. Find the coefficient of kinetic friction.

Answer: C. 5/9

For equal distance from rest, t ∝ 1/√a. Thus a_rough = a_smooth/(1.5)² = (4/9)a_smooth. At 45°, a_rough = g(sin45°−μcos45°), while a_smooth = g sin45°. Hence 1−μ = 4/9, so μ = 5/9.

Q4. A 0.1 kg body starts from rest under a constant force (5î + 10ĵ) N. After 2 s, its position is (2x î + 5y ĵ) m. Find x:y.

Answer: D. 5:4

Acceleration components are 50 and 100 m s⁻². From rest, displacement components after 2 s are 100 m and 200 m. Therefore 2x=100 and 5y=200, giving x:y = 50:40 = 5:4.

Q5. Two blocks of masses 4 kg and 6 kg are connected by a light string passing over a smooth, massless fixed pulley. Taking g = 10 m s⁻², find the tension in the string.

Answer: B. 48 N

Acceleration a=(6−4)g/(6+4)=2 m s⁻². For the 4 kg block, T−4g=4a, so T=40+8=48 N.

Q6. A 2 kg block rests on a smooth 37° wedge of mass 10 kg. The wedge is pulled horizontally to the right by 24 N; the block starts from rest relative to the wedge. How long does it take the block to slide 8.8 m down the incline? Take g = 10 m s⁻² and tan37° = 3/4.

Answer: A. 1.44 s (recalculated from the displayed data)

For the block, a_rel=g sin37°+A cos37°. For the wedge, (M+m)A=F+m a_rel cos37°. With M=10 kg, m=2 kg, F=24 N, sin37°=0.6 and cos37°=0.8, these give a_rel≈8.51 m/s². Then t=√(2s/a_rel)=√(17.6/8.51)≈1.44 s. The originally listed 2 s does not follow from the displayed data.

Q7. An Atwood machine has masses m₁ and m₂ over a light string and smooth fixed pulley. If m₂ is heavier and the acceleration magnitude is g/8, find m₂/m₁.

Answer: D. 9/7

For an Atwood machine, a/g=(m₂−m₁)/(m₂+m₁)=1/8. Let r=m₂/m₁. Then (r−1)/(r+1)=1/8, so 8r−8=r+1, 7r=9 and r=9/7, option D.

Q8. A block takes t seconds to slide down a 45° incline. If the incline is made smooth, the time becomes t/2. Find the coefficient of kinetic friction.

Answer: C. 3/4

For equal distance from rest, acceleration varies inversely as time squared. a_smooth = 4a_rough. Using a_smooth = g sin45° and a_rough = g(sin45°−μcos45°), μ = 3/4.

Q9. A car moves at 54 km h⁻¹ around a curve of radius 20 m. A simple pendulum hangs from its roof. What is tanθ, where θ is the angle the string makes with the vertical?

Answer: C. 1.125

In the car’s frame, tanθ = a_c/g = v²/(rg). With v = 15 m s⁻¹, tanθ = 225/(20×10) = 1.125.

Q10. A 1 kg mass is in equilibrium supported by two strings. T₁ makes 60° and T₂ makes 30° with the horizontal. Find (T₁, T₂), using g = 10 m s⁻².

Answer: B. (5√3, 5) N

Horizontal balance: T₁cos60° = T₂cos30°. Vertical balance: T₁sin60° + T₂sin30° = 10. Solving gives T₁ = 5√3 N and T₂ = 5 N.

Q11. A block slides down a 60° incline with acceleration g/2. Find the coefficient of kinetic friction.

Answer: D. √3−1

Along the incline, g sin60° − μg cos60° = g/2. Therefore μ = (√3−1).

Q12. A 0.5 kg object moves along the x-axis with speed v = 4√x m s⁻¹. Find the force along the x-axis.

Answer: B. 4 N

Since v² = 16x, a = v(dv/dx) = ½d(v²)/dx = 8 m s⁻². Thus F = ma = 4 N.

Q13. A 5 kg object moving at 25 m s⁻¹ is brought to rest by a wall in one experiment in 3 s and in another in 5 s. Which statement is correct?

Answer: B. Impulse is the same but average force differs

The momentum change is the same, so impulse is the same. Average force equals impulse divided by contact time, so it differs. NTA final key: option B.

Q14. An 800 kg car takes a turn on a banked road of radius 300 m and banking angle 30°. If μs = 0.2, find the maximum safe speed (g = 10 m s⁻², √3 = 1.73).

Answer: A. 51.4 m s⁻¹

For maximum speed, friction acts down the slope. Using v²/r = g(tanθ+μ)/(1−μtanθ) gives approximately 51.4 m s⁻¹, matching the NTA final key.

Q15. A 10 kg block slides on a horizontal surface with initial speed 9.8 m s⁻¹. If μ = 0.5 and g = 9.8 m s⁻², find the distance travelled before it stops.

Answer: B. 9.8 m

Deceleration due to friction is μg = 4.9 m s⁻². Using v² = u²−2as, stopping distance s = 9.8²/(2×4.9) = 9.8 m. NTA final key: option B.

Q16. A 200 N body hangs from a tree branch through a uniform chain of mass 10 kg. What force does the branch exert on the chain? Take g = 10 m s⁻².

Answer: C. 300 N

The branch supports the body plus the chain’s weight. Total force = 200 + 10×10 = 300 N.

Q17. A player catches a 150 g cricket ball travelling at 20 m s⁻¹ and brings it to rest in 0.1 s. Find the magnitude of the average force.

Answer: C. 30 N

Average force magnitude = change in momentum/time = (0.15×20)/0.1 = 30 N.

Q18. Three blocks A, B and C of masses 5 kg, 3 kg and 2 kg are connected in a row on a smooth surface. An 80 N force pulls block C. Find tensions T₁ (between A and B) and T₂ (between B and C).

Q19. A coin is at distance r from the centre of a horizontal rotating disc. The coefficient of friction is μ. Find the maximum angular speed so the coin does not slip.

Answer: B. √(μg/r)

Static friction supplies centripetal force: mω²r ≤ μmg. Therefore ω_max = √(μg/r), option B, matching the NTA final key.

Q21. A 100 g stone is tied to a 2 m string and whirled in a horizontal circle. The string withstands a maximum tension of 80 N. If the maximum revolutions per minute are K/π, find K.

Answer: C. 600

At the limiting tension, T = mω²r. With m = 0.1 kg, r = 2 m and T = 80 N, ω = 20 rad s⁻¹. Converting to revolutions per minute gives K = 600, matching the NTA final key.

Q22. Two equal 30 g billiard balls strike a rigid wall at the same speed, 108 km h⁻¹. One hits normally; the other hits at 45° to the normal. Both rebound at the same speed. Find the ratio of impulses along the wall-normal direction, ball (a) : ball (b).

Answer: D. √2:1

Normal impact gives impulse 2mu; oblique impact gives 2mu cos45°. Ratio = 1:cos45° = √2:1. NTA final key: option D.

Q23. A mass m is attached to a light spring of constant k and natural length l. The other end is fixed. The system rotates with angular speed ω in gravity-free space. Find the extension of the spring.

Answer: B. mlω²/(k−mω²)

At steady rotation, kx = mω²(l+x). Rearranging gives x = mlω²/(k−mω²), matching the NTA final key.

Q25. A machine gun fires bullets of mass 10 g at 250 m s⁻¹. An average force of 125 N is needed to keep the gun in position. How many bullets are fired per second?

Answer: B. 50

Recoil force equals momentum delivered per second: F = n m v. Thus n = 125/(0.01×250) = 50 bullets s⁻¹.
