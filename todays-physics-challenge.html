1. Quick theory notes

  • Cv is the molar heat capacity at constant volume; for an ideal gas, supplied heat changes internal energy because the gas does no expansion work.
  • Cp is the molar heat capacity at constant pressure; some supplied heat also does expansion work, so Cp is greater than Cv for an ideal gas.
  • For an ideal gas, Cp and Cv are molar heat capacities and their difference is independent of the gas's molecular structure.
At constant volume: Q = nCvΔT
At constant pressure: Q = nCpΔT
Mayer's relation (ideal gas): Cp − Cv = R
Adiabatic index: γ = Cp/Cv

Use absolute temperature changes in kelvin. These are molar heat capacities, so units are J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹.

Useful ideal-gas results

Monatomic gas: Cv = 3R/2, Cp = 5R/2, γ = 5/3
Rigid diatomic gas at ordinary temperatures: Cv = 5R/2, Cp = 7R/2, γ = 7/5

The diatomic result assumes vibrational modes are not appreciably excited.

2. Worked example

Question: One mole of an ideal gas has Cv = 3R. Find Cp and γ.

Solution: By Mayer's relation, Cp = Cv + R = 4R. Therefore γ = Cp/Cv = 4R/3R = 4/3.

Numerical check: Cp − Cv = 4R − 3R = R ✓

Reveal solutions

2. B — 3R/2. A monatomic ideal gas has three translational degrees of freedom, giving Cv = 3R/2.
3. C — Zero. W = ∫P dV; constant volume means dV = 0 throughout the process.

5. 30-second recap

  • Cp − Cv = R
  • γ = Cp/Cv, so γ > 1 for an ideal gas
  • Constant volume: W = 0 and Q = nCvΔT
  • Constant pressure: Q = nCpΔT

Memory hook: “Pressure is held, extra heat does expansion work — so Cp is greater than Cv.”
