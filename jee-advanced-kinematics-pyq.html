Q1. Official PYQ — projectile, rebound and motion

JEE Advanced 2023 · Paper 1 · Q1

A ball is released from rest on a frictionless curved slide, starting at height h above the top of a building. The building is 3h high. The ball leaves the horizontal end of the slide with velocity u₀ along +x, lands on the ground at horizontal distance d, and rebounds with coefficient of restitution 1/√3 to reach maximum height h₁. Which statements are correct?

Worked verification

Energy on the frictionless slide gives u₀²=2gh, so (A) is true. During the fall through 3h, t=√(6h/g), hence d=u₀t=2√3h. The incoming vertical speed is √(6gh), while horizontal speed is √(2gh), so tanθ=√3 and θ=60°: (C) is true. At the bounce, only the vertical component reverses and is multiplied by e=1/√3; its upward value is √(2gh), so rebound velocity is √(2gh)(x̂+ẑ), not (B). Thus h₁=(2gh)/(2g)=h, and d/h₁=2√3: (D) is true. Correct: A, C, D.

Question source: official JEE Advanced 2023 Paper 1, Physics Q1. Answer independently derived from the stated mechanics. This is a multi-concept original PYQ (energy + projectile + rebound), not a pure-kinematics-only question.

Q2. 1D motion

Single correct

A particle moves along x-axis with x(t)=t³−6t²+9t (SI units). At what time(s) is its instantaneous velocity zero for t ≥ 0?

Worked solution

v=dx/dt=3t²−12t+9=3(t−1)(t−3). Thus v=0 at t=1 s and 3 s. Answer C.

Q3. Position–time graph facts

Multi-correct

For one-dimensional motion, which statements are always correct?

Worked solution

A is the definition of velocity from position; B follows by integrating velocity; D follows by differentiating velocity. An x–t graph's area has no general displacement meaning. Answer A, B, D.

Q4. Projectile — range

Single correct

A projectile is launched from level ground at speed u and angle θ. Neglect air resistance. For a fixed u, the two distinct launch angles that give the same nonzero range are θ and:

Worked solution

R=(u²/g)sin2θ. The same sine for launch angles in (0°,90°) is obtained for complementary angles θ and 90°−θ. Answer B.

Q5. Relative velocity

Single correct

Rain falls vertically at 12 m s⁻¹ relative to the ground. A cyclist moves horizontally at 5 m s⁻¹. The magnitude of rain velocity relative to the cyclist is:

Worked solution

Relative velocity components have perpendicular magnitudes 12 and 5, so magnitude = √(12²+5²)=13 m s⁻¹. Answer B.

Q6. Numerical — vertical motion

Numerical answer

A ball is thrown vertically upward with speed 20 m s⁻¹. Take g=10 m s⁻². Find the total time (in seconds) to return to the launch height.

Worked solution

Total flight time T=2u/g=2(20)/10=4 s. Answer 4.

Q7. Projectile components

Multi-correct

A projectile is launched with initial speed u at angle θ above horizontal. Neglect air resistance. Which are correct?

Worked solution

Standard component equations give T=2u sinθ/g and H=u²sin²θ/(2g). Horizontal acceleration is zero. At the highest point only vertical velocity is zero; horizontal velocity remains u cosθ. Answer A, B, C.

Q8. Two-dimensional motion

Single correct

A particle has position vector r(t)= (3t²)i + (4t)j metres. Its speed at t=1 s is:

Worked solution

v=dr/dt=6t i+4j. At t=1, speed=√(36+16)=√52 m s⁻¹. Answer A.

Q9. Relative motion — catch-up

Numerical answer

Car A passes a point with speed 10 m s⁻¹. At the same instant, car B is 100 m behind and moves in the same direction at 20 m s⁻¹. How long (in seconds) does B take to catch A?

Worked solution

Closing speed=20−10=10 m s⁻¹. Time=100/10=10 s. Answer 10.

Q10. Acceleration from velocity

Single correct

A particle has velocity v=4t−t² m s⁻¹. Its acceleration at t=3 s is:

Worked solution

a=dv/dt=4−2t. At t=3 s, a=−2 m s⁻². Answer C.

Q11. Advanced graph analysis — velocity–time

Original practice · graph

A particle moves along x-axis. Its velocity varies linearly with time: v = −6 m s⁻¹ at t=0 and v = +6 m s⁻¹ at t=4 s. Which statement is correct?

Schematic v–t graph: (0, −6) to (4, +6)

Worked solution

The v–t graph is a straight line crossing v=0 at t=2 s. Signed triangular areas are −½(2)(6) and +½(2)(6), so net displacement is 0. Total distance is 6+6=12 m. Answer C.

Q12. Advanced graph analysis — acceleration–time

Original practice · graph

A particle has initial velocity v(0)=2 m s⁻¹. Its acceleration is +4 m s⁻² for 0≤t<2 s and −2 m s⁻² for 2≤t≤5 s. Find its velocity at t=5 s.

a = +4 from 0–2 s; a = −2 from 2–5 s

Worked solution

From 0 to 2 s, Δv=4×2=8, so v(2)=10 m s⁻¹. From 2 to 5 s, Δv=−2×3=−6. Therefore v(5)=4 m s⁻¹. Correct answer: 4 m s⁻¹.

Q13. Position–time graph — tangents and turning points

Original practice · multi-correct graph

Consider the schematic x–t graph below. Over the shown interval, which statements must be true?

Schematic x–t graph with a local maximum at t₁

Worked solution

The slope of x–t is velocity. It changes from positive to negative through a horizontal tangent, so v(t₁)=0, the direction changes, and x(t) has a local maximum. The acceleration need not be zero; for a smooth local maximum it may be negative. Correct: A, B, D.

Q14. Average acceleration from perpendicular velocity change

IIT-JEE 1982 · reconstructed

A particle's velocity changes from 5 m s⁻¹ east to 5 m s⁻¹ north in 10 s. Find the magnitude of its average acceleration in m s⁻².

Worked solution

Take east as +x and north as +y. Δv = (−5, +5) m s⁻¹, so |Δv| = √(25+25)=5√2 m s⁻¹. Therefore |a_avg|=5√2/10=1/√2≈0.707 m s⁻².

IIT-JEE 1984 · True/False format

A projectile is launched from level ground and follows a parabolic trajectory under uniform gravity, with no air resistance. Where is its speed minimum?

Worked solution

The horizontal velocity u cosθ remains constant while the vertical component u sinθ−gt decreases to zero at the apex and then reverses. Thus speed is minimum at the highest point (for a non-vertical launch, the minimum is |u cosθ|). Answer B.

Q19. Four people chasing one another at square corners

IIT-JEE 1984 · reconstructed

Four people start simultaneously at the corners of a square of side d. Each moves at constant speed v directly towards the next person, cyclically. By symmetry, they meet at the centre. Find the meeting time in terms of d and v; enter the numerical coefficient multiplying d/v (to three decimals).

Worked solution

At any instant the square shrinks self-similarly. A person's velocity towards the centre has magnitude v cos45°=v/√2. The initial distance from a corner to the centre is d/√2, so time=(d/√2)/(v/√2)=d/v. Hence the coefficient is 1.000. (Use t=d/v; the numeric entry should be 1.)

Q20. Boat crossing — shortest path

IIT-JEE 1988 · reconstructed

A boat's speed in still water is 5 km h⁻¹. It crosses a 1 km wide river along the shortest possible path in 15 minutes. Find the river speed.

Worked solution

Crossing time is 0.25 h, so the across-river component of the ground velocity is 1/0.25=4 km h⁻¹. For the shortest path, the ground velocity is perpendicular to the banks; the boat's 5 km h⁻¹ velocity relative to water must cancel the current component. By the velocity triangle, current speed = √(5²−4²)=3 km h⁻¹. Answer C.

Q24. Official PYQ — projectile through a specified point

JEE Advanced 2026 · Paper 1 · Q6

A particle is thrown with speed v from point O at angle θ with the horizontal so that it passes through P, which is 1 m above O and 5 m horizontally from O. If the acceleration due to gravity is g, which statements are correct?

Worked solution

Projectile trajectory: y = x tanθ − gx²/(2v²cos²θ). At P(5,1): 1 = 5tanθ − 25g/(2v²cos²θ).

(A) For θ=45°, 1=5−25g/v², hence v²=25g/4 and v=5√g/2. True.

(B) At 45°, range R=v²/g=25/4=6.25 m, so apex x=R/2=3.125 m, before P at x=5 m. True.

(C) For θ=30°, solving gives v²≈8.83g and R≈7.65 m; apex is at R/2≈3.825 m, before P, not after. False.

(D) tanθ=1/5 makes the straight line OP itself the no-gravity trajectory line; gravity bends the projectile below it, so no finite v can pass through P at this exact angle. False. Correct: A and B.

Q25. Original historical PYQ — acceleration, retardation and v–t graph

IIT-JEE 1978 · Subjective

A car accelerates from rest at constant rate α for some time, then decelerates at constant rate β to come to rest. If the total elapsed time is t, find (i) the maximum velocity reached and (ii) the total distance travelled.

Worked solution

Let the acceleration time be t₁ and retardation time be t₂. At peak speed V, V=αt₁=βt₂, and t₁+t₂=t. Therefore V(1/α+1/β)=t, giving V=αβt/(α+β). The v–t graph is a triangle with base t and height V, so total distance s=½Vt=αβt²/[2(α+β)].

Q26. Original PYQ — speed of shadow tip relative to a person

JEE Advanced 2023 · Paper 1

A person of height 1.6 m walks away from a 4 m high lamp post along a straight horizontal path at 60 cm s⁻¹. Find the speed, in cm s⁻¹, of the tip of the person's shadow relative to the person.

Worked solution

Let x be the person's distance from the lamp and l the shadow length. By similar triangles, 4/(x+l)=1.6/l, so 4l=1.6x+1.6l and l=(2/3)x. Thus dl/dt=(2/3)(60)=40 cm s⁻¹. The tip moves away from the person at the rate dl/dt, so the required relative speed is 40 cm s⁻¹.

Q27. Original PYQ — projectile interception at two angle pairs

JEE Advanced 2024 · Paper 2

A ball is projected from (0,0) with speed v₀ at angle θ₀ above the +x-axis. At the same time, a stone is projected from (L,0) at angle (180°−θ₁), with a suitable speed, so that it hits the ball. For the same v₀, the collision times are T₁ when (θ₀,θ₁)=(45°,45°) and T₂ when (θ₀,θ₁)=(60°,30°). Find (T₁/T₂)².

Worked solution

Let the stone speed be u. Equality of vertical coordinates at collision gives v₀sinθ₀ = u sinθ₁, so u=v₀sinθ₀/sinθ₁. Horizontally, v₀cosθ₀·T = L − u cosθ₁·T, hence T=L/[v₀(cosθ₀+sinθ₀ cotθ₁)].

For (45°,45°), the denominator factor is √2, so T₁=L/(√2v₀). For (60°,30°), it is 1/2+(√3/2)√3=2, so T₂=L/(2v₀). Therefore (T₁/T₂)²=(2/√2)²=2. Answer: 2.

Q28. Two-dimensional path y = x²/2

JEE Advanced 2020 · Paper 2 · Q12

A particle starts at the origin with speed 1 m s⁻¹ and follows the path y=x²/2. Its acceleration components are aₓ and aᵧ. Which statements are correct?

Worked solution

From y=x²/2, vᵧ=xvₓ and aᵧ=vₓ²+xaₓ. At the origin x=0 and initial speed 1 is along +x, so vₓ=1 and aᵧ=1 independent of aₓ. If aₓ=0, vₓ remains 1, x=t, and vᵧ=x=t; at t=1, tanθ=vᵧ/vₓ=1. Thus A–D are correct.

Q29. Relative motion of two airplanes

JEE Advanced 2014 · Paper 1 · Q11

Two airplanes move in the same vertical plane at 30° and 60° to the horizontal. Airplane A has speed 100√3 m s⁻¹. Initially, B is 500 m from A, and the velocity of B relative to A is perpendicular to A's direction. Find the time (s) after which A just escapes being hit by B.

Q30. Two projectiles fired from a hill

IIT-JEE 1996 · Subjective

Two guns at the top of a 10 m hill fire projectiles with the same speed 5√3 m s⁻¹. One fires horizontally; the other fires at 60° above the horizontal after a time delay. They collide in air. Taking the origin at the foot of the hill, find the firing time interval and collision coordinates. Use g=10 m s⁻².

Worked solution

The inclined shot must be fired first. Let τ be its flight time to collision and t be the time since the horizontal shot is fired. Horizontal positions give (u cos60°)τ = u t, so t=τ/2. Equating vertical positions gives u sin60°·τ − (g/2)τ² = −(g/2)(τ/2)², hence τ=8u sin60°/(3g)=2 s for u=5√3 m s⁻¹ and g=10 m s⁻². Therefore the firing interval is Δ=τ−t=1 s. Collision coordinates: x=u cos60°·τ=5√3 m; y=10+u sin60°·τ−(g/2)τ²=5 m. Answer: interval 1 s; P=(5√3 m, 5 m).

Q31. Ball thrown inside an accelerating train

IIT-JEE 2011 · Paper 2 · Q36

A train accelerates uniformly. A boy throws a ball forward at 10 m s⁻¹ relative to the train, at 60° above horizontal. To catch it at the initial height, he must move 1.15 m forward inside the train. Find the train's acceleration in m s⁻². Take g=10 m s⁻².

Worked solution

Flight time T=2u sin60°/g=√3 s. Relative to the train, the ball's horizontal displacement at return is u cos60°·T=5√3≈8.66 m; the boy advances 1.15 m relative to the train as specified in the original frame relation. In the ground frame, the horizontal displacement difference is 1.15=(u cos60°)T−(aT²/2). Thus a=2[(5√3)(√3)−1.15]/3=2(15−1.15)/3≈9.23, inconsistent with the official answer if the relative displacement is interpreted this way. The source's stated geometry and timing convention yields a=5 m s⁻²; use the linked official-style solution to check the frame relation.

Official indexed answer: 5 m s⁻². Solution derivation should be checked against the original diagram before this item is used for grading.

Q32. Projectile after bouncing with energy loss

JEE Advanced 2018 · Paper 2 · Q8

A projectile is launched at 45° and reaches a maximum height of 120 m. At its first impact with the ground it loses half its kinetic energy. Immediately after the bounce, its velocity makes 30° with the horizontal. Find the new maximum height in metres.

Worked solution

Initial maximum height H₀=u²sin²45°/(2g)=u²/(4g)=120 m, so u²=480g. Losing half the kinetic energy means the speed after bounce is u/√2. The new vertical component is (u/√2)sin30°=u/(2√2). Thus H=(u²/8)/(2g)=u²/(16g)=30 m. Answer 30.

Q33. Projectile enters a region with different effective gravity

JEE Advanced 2022 · Paper 1 · Q8

A projectile has range d under gravity g. At the highest point it enters a region where effective gravity becomes g′=g/0.81, with the same horizontal velocity and no vertical velocity discontinuity. Its new range is d′=nd. Find n.

Worked solution

Original range d=v²sin2θ/g. The first half-range remains d/2. At the apex the vertical speed is zero; the time for the second half is set by the new gravity g′=g/0.81, so the second horizontal distance is 0.9(d/2). Therefore d′=d/2+0.9d/2=0.95d, giving n=0.95.

Q37. Average velocity over repeated projectile rebounds

JEE Advanced 2019 · Paper 2

A ball is projected from ground at angle θ with initial speed u₀. Its average-velocity magnitude for the first flight is V₁. At each bounce it leaves the ground at the same angle, but its speed is reduced to u₀/α, then u₀/α², and so on. If the magnitude of average velocity over the entire sequence is 0.8V₁, find α.

Worked solution

For each flight, horizontal range scales as speed squared and flight time scales as speed. The total horizontal displacement is proportional to u₀²[1+α⁻²+α⁻⁴+…]=u₀²/(1−α⁻²), while total time is proportional to u₀[1+α⁻¹+α⁻²+…]=u₀/(1−α⁻¹). Hence the overall average velocity relative to V₁ is (1−α⁻¹)/(1−α⁻²)=α/(α+1). Set α/(α+1)=0.8, so α=4. Answer 4.

Q39. Distance covered in successive seconds

IIT-JEE 2004 Screening

A particle starts from rest and slides down a frictionless fixed incline with constant acceleration. Let Sₙ be the distance travelled during the interval from t=n−1 s to t=n s. What is Sₙ/Sₙ₊₁?

Worked solution

For constant acceleration a from rest, x(t)=½at². Therefore Sₙ=x(n)−x(n−1)=½a[n²−(n−1)²]=½a(2n−1). Similarly Sₙ₊₁=½a(2n+1). Ratio=(2n−1)/(2n+1). Answer B.

Q40. Position as a function of time under constant force

IIT-JEE 1979 · Subjective

A particle moves in one dimension under a constant force, and its displacement x (metres) and time t (seconds) are related by t=√x+3. Find (i) the displacement when its velocity is zero, and (ii) the work done by the force during the first 6 seconds.

Worked solution

From t=√x+3, x=(t−3)². Hence v=dx/dt=2(t−3), which is zero at t=3 s; then x=0. At t=0, v=−6 m s⁻¹; at t=6 s, v=+6 m s⁻¹. Since the force is constant, work done equals change in kinetic energy: W=½m(v_f²−v_i²)=½m(36−36)=0. Answers: (i) 0 m; (ii) 0 J.

Q41. Expanding sphere — surface speed and radius

JEE Advanced 2017 · Paper 2 · Motion index (related rates)

A sphere expands while its total mass remains constant and its density stays uniform. The fractional rate of change of density is constant. The speed v of a point on its surface is proportional to which quantity?

Worked solution

For fixed mass M, density ρ=3M/(4πR³). Logarithmic differentiation gives (1/ρ)dρ/dt=−3(1/R)dR/dt=−3v/R. The fractional density rate is constant, so v/R is constant; therefore v∝R. Answer C.

Q42. Train motion along the equator

IIT-JEE 1985 · Motion index (rotation-mixed)

Two identical trains move along the equator in opposite directions with equal speeds relative to Earth. True or false: they exert the same pressure on the rails.

Worked explanation

The train's inertial-frame speed combines with Earth's rotational speed. For eastward motion the speeds add; for westward motion they subtract. Required centripetal acceleration and normal reaction therefore differ, so the statement is false. This is a rotation/relative-motion mixed question, not pure kinematics.
