Q1. Car acceleration and retardation

IIT-JEE 1978

A car starts from rest, accelerates at a constant rate, then decelerates at a constant rate until it stops. If the total time is t, determine its maximum speed and total distance travelled in terms of the acceleration α and retardation β.

Solution

Let the peak speed be V. Acceleration time is V/α and retardation time is V/β, so V(1/α+1/β)=t. Thus V=αβt/(α+β). Distance is the triangular area under the v–t graph: s=Vt/2=αβt²/[2(α+β)].

Q2. Position–time graph: physical possibility

IIT-JEE 1979

A position–time graph contains a vertical segment, assigning two positions to the same instant. Can it represent the motion of a single particle? Explain.

Solution

No. Position must be single-valued at each time for a single particle. A vertical segment assigns two distinct positions to the same instant, so it is not a physically valid x–t graph.

Q4. Average acceleration from a velocity-vector change

IIT-JEE 1982

A particle's velocity changes from 5 m s⁻¹ east to 5 m s⁻¹ north in 10 s. Find the magnitude of its average acceleration (m s⁻²).

Solution

Δv has perpendicular components −5 and +5 m s⁻¹, so |Δv|=5√2. Average acceleration magnitude=5√2/10=1/√2≈0.707 m s⁻².

Q5. Vertical throws: equal speed on return

IIT-JEE 1983

Two balls of different masses are thrown vertically upward with the same initial speed. Neglecting air resistance, when each passes the launch point on its way down, what can be said about their speeds?

Solution

With no air resistance, mechanical energy is conserved. At the launch height the speed equals the initial speed, independent of mass. Answer A.

Q6. Circular motion: half a revolution

IIT-JEE 1983

A particle moves on a circle of radius R. Find its displacement magnitude and distance travelled in half a period.

Solution

Displacement magnitude is the diameter, 2R. Distance travelled is half the circumference, πR.

Q7. Projectile speed at the top

IIT-JEE 1984 · True/False style

A projectile follows a parabolic path under uniform gravity with no air resistance. Its speed is minimum at the top of the trajectory. Choose the correct response.

Solution

At the top, the vertical velocity component is zero while the horizontal component remains constant, so the speed is minimum. Answer B (True).

Q8. Four people chasing around a square

IIT-JEE 1984

Four people begin at the corners of a square of side d. Each moves at constant speed v directly toward the next person in cyclic order. Find the time when they meet.

Solution

By symmetry, the square shrinks self-similarly. Each person's inward radial speed is v cos45°=v/√2; initial distance from a corner to the centre is d/√2. Therefore meeting time=(d/√2)/(v/√2)=d/v.

Q9. Boat crossing: shortest possible path

IIT-JEE 1988

A boat moves at 5 km h⁻¹ in still water and crosses a river 1 km wide along the shortest possible path in 15 minutes. Find the river current speed.

Solution

Across-river ground speed=1/0.25=4 km h⁻¹. For the shortest path, the boat's 5 km h⁻¹ velocity relative to water cancels the current component; hence current speed=√(5²−4²)=3 km h⁻¹. Answer C.

Q10. Two-dimensional motion: x=a cos(pt), y=b sin(pt)

IIT-JEE 1999

A particle has x(t)=a cos(pt), y(t)=b sin(pt), where a, b and p are positive constants. Which acceleration-component relations are correct?

Solution

Twice differentiating each coordinate gives x''=−p²x and y''=−p²y. Correct answers A and B.

Q11. Initial and final rest with unit displacement

IIT-JEE 1993

A particle moves on the x-axis from rest at x=0, t=0 and comes to rest at x=1 m, t=1 s. No other information about intermediate motion is given. Which statements are necessarily true?

Solution

To start at rest, achieve positive net displacement and return to rest, velocity must become positive and later decrease to zero. Hence acceleration must be positive at some time and negative at some time. It cannot stay strictly positive for the entire interval, and no fixed upper bound of 2 follows. Correct: A, C, D.

Q12. Maximum speed from an acceleration–time graph

IIT-JEE 2004 Screening

A body starts from rest. Its acceleration decreases linearly from 10 m s⁻² at t=0 to zero at t=11 s. Find the maximum speed.

Solution

Change in velocity is the area under the a–t graph: Δv=½×11×10=55 m s⁻¹. Answer 55.

Q13. Distance in successive one-second intervals

IIT-JEE 2004 Screening

A particle starts from rest and slides down a frictionless incline with constant acceleration. If Sₙ is the distance travelled from t=n−1 s to t=n s, find Sₙ/Sₙ₊₁.

Solution

x(t)=½at², so Sₙ=½a[n²−(n−1)²]=½a(2n−1), and Sₙ₊₁=½a(2n+1). Ratio=(2n−1)/(2n+1). Answer B.

Q15. Average velocity over repeated projectile bounces

JEE Advanced 2019 · Paper 2

A ball is projected from the ground at angle θ with speed u₀. After each bounce it leaves at the same angle, with speed reduced by a factor α. If the magnitude of average velocity over the entire sequence is 0.8 times the first-flight average-velocity magnitude, find α.

Solution

Ranges scale with speed squared and flight times with speed. For speed ratio r=1/α, total horizontal displacement and total time form geometric series, giving V̄/V₁=1/(1+r)=α/(α+1). Setting this to 0.8 gives α=4. Answer 4.

Q16. Projectile after losing kinetic energy at impact

JEE Advanced 2018 · Paper 2

A projectile launched at 45° reaches maximum height 120 m. On its first impact it loses half its kinetic energy; immediately after the bounce its velocity makes 30° with the horizontal. Find the next maximum height (m).

Solution

Original H=u²/(4g)=120, so u²=480g. Half the kinetic energy remaining gives rebound speed u/√2. Vertical component is u/(2√2), so H'=(u²/8)/(2g)=u²/(16g)=30 m. Answer 30.

Q17. Projectile enters a region with changed effective gravity

JEE Advanced 2022 · Paper 1

A projectile has range d under gravity g. At the highest point it enters a region where the effective gravity becomes g/0.81. Find n if the new total range is d′=nd.

Solution

The first half-range is d/2. After the apex, the vertical fall time scales by √(g/g′)=0.9, so the second horizontal segment is 0.9(d/2). Thus d′=0.95d and n=0.95.

Q18. Speed of a shadow tip relative to the person

JEE Advanced 2023 · Paper 1

A person of height 1.6 m walks away from a 4 m lamp post at 60 cm s⁻¹. Find the speed of the tip of the shadow relative to the person (cm s⁻¹).

Solution

Similar triangles give 4/(x+l)=1.6/l, so l=(2/3)x. Therefore dl/dt=(2/3)(60)=40 cm s⁻¹. Answer 40.

Q20. 2D path y=x²/2

JEE Advanced 2020 · Paper 2

A particle starts at the origin with speed 1 m s⁻¹ and follows y=x²/2. Which statements about acceleration components and velocity at t=0 are correct?

Solution

From y=x²/2, vᵧ=xvₓ and aᵧ=vₓ²+xaₓ. At x=0, initial speed is along +x, so aᵧ=1. If aₓ=0, vₓ=1, x=t and vᵧ=t; at t=1, the velocity is at 45°. Correct A, B and D.

Q22. Two balls thrown inside an accelerating rocket

JEE Advanced 2014 · Paper 1

A rocket accelerates at 2 m s⁻² in gravity-free space. Its chamber is 4 m long. Balls are thrown simultaneously from opposite ends at 0.3 m s⁻¹ and 0.2 m s⁻¹ toward one another, relative to the rocket. Find the meeting time (s).

Solution

In the rocket frame both balls share the same pseudo-acceleration, so relative acceleration is zero. Closing speed=0.3+0.2=0.5 m s⁻¹. Time=4/0.5=8 s. Answer 8.

Q23. Ball thrown inside an accelerating train

IIT-JEE 2011 · Paper 2

A train accelerates uniformly. A boy throws a ball forward at 10 m s⁻¹ relative to the train at 60° to the horizontal and moves forward 1.15 m inside the train to catch it at the launch height. Take g=10 m s⁻². Find the train acceleration (m s⁻²).

Solution

Flight time T=2(10)sin60°/10=√3 s. In the train frame the ball's relative horizontal displacement at return is u cos60°·T−½aT². Equate this to 1.15 m: 1.15=5√3−1.5a, giving a≈5.01 m s⁻², i.e. 5 m s⁻² at the stated precision. Answer 5.

Q24. Projectile passing through a specified point

JEE Advanced 2026 · Paper 1

A particle is projected from O at speed v and angle θ so it passes through P, which is 1 m above O and 5 m horizontally from O. Which statements are correct?

Solution

Use y=x tanθ−gx²/(2v²cos²θ) at (x,y)=(5,1). For θ=45°, 1=5−25g/v², so v=5√g/2; the apex is at x=R/2=25/8 m, before P. For θ=30°, the apex is also before P. If tanθ=1/5, the no-gravity straight-line height at x=5 is already 1 m; gravity lowers the trajectory, so no finite speed can pass through P. Correct A and B.

Q25. River crossing in minimum time

IIT-JEE 1983

A river flows west to east at 5 m min⁻¹. A swimmer can move at 10 m min⁻¹ in still water and wants to cross in the shortest possible time. In which direction should the swimmer aim relative to the river bank?

Solution

Crossing time depends on the velocity component perpendicular to the bank. That component is maximized by aiming directly across; the current changes downstream drift, not crossing time. Answer A.

Q26. Relative motion viewed from a moving train

IIT-JEE 2008 · Paper 2

Statement I: From a fast-moving train, nearby objects appear to move opposite to the train while distant objects may appear stationary. Statement II: The object's velocity relative to an observer is v_object−v_observer. Choose the correct option.

Solution

Relative velocity is the vector difference v_object−v_observer. Nearby objects show a stronger apparent backward motion than distant objects. Both statements are true; answer B.

Q27. Expanding sphere: surface speed

JEE Advanced 2017 · Paper 2 · related rates

A sphere of instantaneous radius R expands at constant mass with uniform density. The fractional rate of change of density is constant. The speed v of a point on the surface is proportional to which quantity?

Solution

For fixed mass, ρ∝R⁻³. Logarithmic differentiation gives (1/ρ)dρ/dt=−3v/R. A constant fractional density-change rate therefore means v/R is constant, so v∝R. Answer C.
