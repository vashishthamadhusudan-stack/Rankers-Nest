Q1. Maximum velocity from the acceleration–time graph

IIT-JEE 2004 Screening · PYQ

A body starts from rest at time t = 0. The acceleration–time graph is shown. The maximum velocity attained by the body will be:

Answer: B — 55 m/s

Velocity gain is the area under the positive a–t graph: Δv = ½ × 11 × 10 = 55 m/s. Since the body starts from rest, maximum velocity is 55 m/s. The redrawn line and labels were checked against the published 2004 question figure.

Q2. Is the shown time-variation of position physically possible?

IIT-JEE 1979 · subjective PYQ, adapted

Original question (wording lightly normalized): “Answer the following giving reason in brief: Is the time variation of position, shown in the figure observed in nature?”

Answer: D — Not possible

A horizontal line on the original figure corresponds to one time but intersects the curve at two positions A and B. One particle cannot occupy both positions at one instant. This SVG is a redraw checked against the published 1979 figure, not a facsimile.

Q3. Velocity–time graph: displacement

Graph drill · practice

A particle's velocity changes linearly from +8 m/s at t=0 to −4 m/s at t=6 s. What is its net displacement in the 6 s interval?

Answer: B (12 m)

The signed area under the v–t graph is displacement. Average velocity for a linear change is (8 + (−4))/2 = 2 m/s, so displacement = 2 × 6 = 12 m.

Q4. Position–time slope

Graph drill · practice

Two straight segments on an x–t graph have slopes +3 m/s and −2 m/s. What do these slopes represent?

Answer: C

The slope of an x–t graph is velocity. Positive slope means positive velocity; negative slope means motion in the negative x direction.

Q5. Velocity–time graph: total distance

Graph drill · practice

Velocity is +4 m/s from t=0 to 3 s and −2 m/s from t=3 to 7 s. Find the total distance travelled.

Answer: 20 m

Distance adds magnitudes of areas: |4|×3 + |−2|×4 = 12+8=20 m. Correct numerical answer is 20 m.

Q6. Acceleration–time graph and velocity

Graph drill · practice

A particle starts from rest. Its acceleration is +2 m/s² for 0–4 s and −1 m/s² for 4–8 s. Select all correct statements.

Answer: A and C

From 0–4 s, Δv=2×4=8 m/s. From 4–8 s, Δv=−1×4=−4 m/s, so final velocity is 4 m/s. It remains positive, so no reversal occurs.

Q7. Projectile through P — select all correct statements

JEE Advanced 2026 · Paper 1, Q6

A particle is projected from O at angle θ and passes through P, 5 m horizontally away and 1 m above O. Select all correct statements.

Answer: A and B

Trajectory: y=x tanθ−gx²/(2v²cos²θ). At P(5,1), for θ=45°, v²=25g/4 so A is true. The range is v²/g=6.25 m, so the apex is at x=3.125 m, before P; B is true. For θ=30°, solving the trajectory gives an apex before P, so C is false. If θ=tan⁻¹(1/5), the launch line is exactly along OP. Gravity bends the trajectory below that launch line, so the projectile cannot pass through P at that angle for any finite speed. The required speed would tend to infinity; option D is false.

Q8. Average acceleration when velocity turns through 90°

IIT-JEE 1982 · PYQ, options adapted

A particle is moving eastwards with a velocity of 5 m/s. In 10 s the velocity changes to 5 m/s northwards. The average acceleration in this time is:

Answer: B

Δv = (−5 i + 5 j) m/s, so |Δv| = 5√2 m/s and its direction is north-west. The average acceleration magnitude is |Δv|/10 = 1/√2 m/s².

Q9. Successive-second distances

IIT-JEE 2004 · PYQ

A particle starts from rest and slides down a frictionless incline with constant acceleration. If Sₙ is the distance travelled from t=n−1 s to t=n s, find Sₙ/Sₙ₊₁.

Answer: B

x(t)=½at². Therefore Sₙ=½a[n²−(n−1)²]=½a(2n−1), and Sₙ₊₁=½a(2n+1). Their ratio is (2n−1)/(2n+1).

Q10. River crossing in the shortest time

Relative-motion drill · practice, not PYQ

A river is flowing from west to east at 5 metres per minute. A man on the south bank can swim at 10 metres per minute in still water. To cross the river in the shortest possible time, he should swim in which direction?

Answer: A — Due north

For minimum crossing time, maximize the swimmer's velocity component perpendicular to the banks. That component is greatest when he swims due north. The current changes downstream drift, not the northward component.

Q11. Motion on an elliptical path

IIT-JEE 1999 Screening · PYQ, options adapted

The coordinates of a particle moving in a plane are x(t)=a cos(pt) and y(t)=b sin(pt), where a, b (b<a), and p are positive constants of appropriate dimensions. Select all correct statements.

Answer: A and B

From x=a cos(pt), y=b sin(pt), we get x²/a²+y²/b²=1, an ellipse. At t=π/(2p), velocity is −ap î while acceleration is −bp² ĵ; these are perpendicular. Acceleration is −p²r, directed toward the centre, not a focus. The quarter-ellipse arc length is not generally a, so D is false.

Q12. Total distance from a velocity graph

Graph drill · practice

A particle moves with velocity +3 m/s from 0–4 s, then −2 m/s from 4–7 s. Find total distance travelled.

Answer: 18 m

Total distance = |3|×4 + |−2|×3 = 12+6=18 m.

Q13. Velocity–time graph: direction change

Graph drill · practice

Velocity decreases linearly from +6 m/s at t=0 to −6 m/s at t=6 s. At what time does the particle reverse direction?

Answer: C

Velocity changes linearly and symmetrically from +6 to −6 m/s. It crosses zero halfway through the 6 s interval, at t=3 s.

Q14. Projectile after impact

JEE Advanced 2018 Paper 2 · PYQ

A projectile launched at 45° reaches maximum height 120 m. At first impact it loses half its kinetic energy; immediately after rebound its velocity makes 30° with horizontal. Find the next maximum height in metres.

Answer: 30 m

Original maximum height H=u²/(4g)=120, so u²=480g. Half kinetic energy remains, giving rebound speed u/√2. Its vertical component is u/(2√2), so H′=(u²/8)/(2g)=u²/(16g)=30 m.

Q15. Shadow tip speed

JEE Advanced 2023 Paper 1 · PYQ

A person of height 1.6 m is walking away from a lamp post of height 4 m along a straight path on flat ground. The lamp post and the person are always perpendicular to the ground. If the person’s speed is 60 cm/s, find the speed of the tip of the person’s shadow on the ground with respect to the person, in cm/s.

Answer: 40 cm/s

Let x be the person's distance from the post and l the shadow length. Similar triangles give 4/(x+l)=1.6/l, so l=(2/3)x. Hence dl/dt=(2/3)(60)=40 cm/s.

Q16. Is the projectile’s speed minimum at the top?

IIT-JEE 1984 · PYQ

True or False: A projectile fired from the ground follows a parabolic path, and its speed is minimum at the top of its path (neglect air resistance).

Answer: A — True

At the highest point, the vertical component of velocity is zero while the horizontal component remains constant. Therefore the speed is minimum there.

Q17. Same launch speed, different masses

IIT-JEE 1983 · PYQ

Two balls of different masses are thrown vertically upward with the same initial speed. Neglecting air resistance, they pass the point of projection during their downward motion with the same speed. Is the statement true or false?

Answer: A — True

With air resistance neglected, both balls have the same acceleration g and conserve mechanical energy. On returning to the launch height, each has the same speed magnitude as its initial speed, independent of mass.

Q18. Projectile enters region with changed gravity

JEE Advanced 2022 Paper 1 · PYQ

A projectile is fired from horizontal ground with speed v and projection angle θ. When acceleration due to gravity is g, the range is d. At the highest point in its trajectory, the projectile enters a different region where the effective acceleration due to gravity is g′ = g/0.81. The new range is d′ = nd. Find n.

Answer: 0.95

The first half-range is d/2. In the second region, fall time scales by √(g/g′)=0.9, so the second horizontal segment is 0.9d/2. Thus d′=0.95d.

Q19. Position, velocity and work under constant force

Practice · not a PYQ

For a particle of mass m moving in one dimension, its position is x=(t−3)² metres for 0≤t≤6 s. Find (i) the position when its velocity is zero, and (ii) the net work done by the constant force over the full 6-second interval.

Answer: (i) 0 m; (ii) 0 J

v=dx/dt=2(t−3), so v=0 at t=3 s and x=0. At t=0 and t=6 s, the velocities are −6 and +6 m/s, so the speed magnitudes are equal. By the work–energy theorem, net work is ΔK=½m(vf²−vi²)=0. This is a corrected practice variant; the indexed 1979 transcription t=√x+3 is not consistent with a real position for the entire first six seconds, so it is not presented here as a verified original.

Q20. Meeting time for four people chasing around a square

IIT-JEE 1984 · PYQ

Four people start at the corners of a square of side d. Each moves with constant speed v directly towards the next person in cyclic order. Express the meeting time as a multiple of d/v; enter the numerical multiplier.

Answer: 1, so t=d/v

Each person's inward velocity component is v/√2 and the initial corner-to-centre distance is d/√2. Thus meeting time=(d/√2)/(v/√2)=d/v.

Q21. Assertion–Reason: slope of an x–t graph

A–R · practice

Assertion (A): The slope of a position–time graph at an instant gives the instantaneous velocity.

Reason (R): Instantaneous velocity is the limit of average velocity as the time interval approaches zero.

Answer: A

Average velocity is Δx/Δt. In the limit Δt→0, this becomes dx/dt, the tangent slope of the x–t graph; therefore R correctly explains A.

Q22. Assertion–Reason: area under a v–t graph

A–R · practice

Assertion (A): The signed area under a velocity–time graph equals displacement.

Reason (R): Velocity is the time derivative of displacement, so integrating velocity over time gives the change in displacement.

Answer: A

Since v=dx/dt, integrating v over the interval gives Δx. The signed area under the v–t graph therefore equals displacement, so R correctly explains A.

Q23. Maximum speed and distance for a car

IIT-JEE 1978 · PYQ, options added for auto-check

A car accelerates from rest at a constant rate α for some time, after which it decelerates at a constant rate β to come to rest. If the total time lapse is t seconds, evaluate (i) the maximum velocity reached and (ii) the total distance travelled.

Answer: A

Let the acceleration and deceleration times be t1 and t2. Since peak speed is v, t1=v/α and t2=v/β. Thus t=v(1/α+1/β), giving vmax=αβt/(α+β). The v–t graph is a triangle, so s=½vt=αβt²/[2(α+β)].

Q24. Displacement and distance in half a revolution

Circular-motion drill · practice, not PYQ

A particle moves in a circle of radius R. In half the period of revolution, its displacement magnitude and distance covered are:

Answer: A

After half a revolution, the particle is at the diametrically opposite point. Displacement magnitude is 2R and distance travelled is half the circumference, πR.

Q25. Pressure exerted by trains on the equator

IIT-JEE 1985 · True/False PYQ (wording clarified)

Two identical trains travel on rails along the equator at equal speeds relative to Earth's surface, but in opposite directions. Is it necessarily true that they exert equal normal force on the rails?

Answer: B — No

Eastward and westward trains have different inertial-frame tangential speeds because Earth's rotation adds to one direction and subtracts from the other. Their required centripetal accelerations and normal reactions therefore differ. The original IIT-JEE 1985 statement is source-matched. The wording here clarifies that the trains have equal speeds relative to Earth’s surface; the direction of Earth’s rotation makes their inertial-frame speeds different, so the normal reactions are not equal.

Q26. River velocity from shortest-path crossing

IIT-JEE 1988 · PYQ

A boat has speed 5 km/h in still water and crosses a river of width 1 km along the shortest possible path in 15 minutes. Find the speed of the river water in km/h.

Answer: 3 km/h

The across-river resultant speed is width/time = 1/(15/60) = 4 km/h. To travel along the shortest path, the boat's upstream component cancels the current. With boat speed 5 km/h, current speed = √(5²−4²)=3 km/h.

Q27. What can be inferred about acceleration?

IIT-JEE 1993 · multi-correct PYQ

A particle of mass m moves along the x-axis. It starts from rest at t=0 at x=0 and comes to rest at t=1 at x=1. No other information is available about its motion at intermediate times (0<t<1). If α denotes its instantaneous acceleration, select the correct statements.

Answer: A and C

Since v(0)=v(1)=0 but displacement is 1 m, velocity must be positive over part of the interval, so acceleration cannot remain positive throughout: A is true. If |a| were everywhere less than 4 m/s², the largest possible positive velocity pulse between the zero-velocity endpoints is bounded by a triangle of base 1 s and height less than 2 m/s, whose area is less than 1 m. That contradicts the given displacement. Therefore |a| must reach at least 4 m/s² somewhere: C is true. B is false, and D is false because C provides an additional definite conclusion. Original multi-correct marking: +2 for the exact correct set, −0.5 for an incorrect non-empty selection.

Q28. Relative motion: statement and reason

IIT-JEE 2008 Paper 2 · PYQ

Statement 1: For an observer looking through the window of a fast-moving train, nearby objects appear to move opposite to the train, while distant objects appear stationary.

Statement 2: If observer and object velocities in the laboratory frame are vₒ and vᵦ, the object's velocity relative to the observer is vᵦ−vₒ.

Select the correct relationship between the statements.

Answer: B

The relative-velocity formula in Statement 2 is true. Statement 1 concerns apparent angular motion: nearby objects show larger angular displacement while distant objects appear nearly stationary. The general relative-velocity formula does not by itself explain that perspective effect.

Q29. Acceleration of a moving train

IIT-JEE 2011 Paper 2 · PYQ

A train moves in a straight line with constant acceleration a. A boy standing in the train throws a ball forward with speed 10 m/s at an angle of 60° to the horizontal. To catch the ball again at the initial height, the boy must move forward by 1.15 m inside the train. Find a in m/s². Use g = 10 m/s².

Answer: 5 m/s²

The flight time is T=2u sin60°/g=√3 s. Relative to the train, the ball's horizontal displacement is u cos60°·T − ½aT². Setting this equal to 1.15 m gives a≈5 m/s² (the published numerical answer is 5).

Q30. Two balls in an accelerating rocket

JEE Advanced 2014 Paper 1 · PYQ, wording normalized

A rocket moves in gravity-free space with constant acceleration 2 m/s² along +x. Its chamber is 4 m long. A ball is thrown from the left end in +x at 0.3 m/s relative to the rocket, while simultaneously another ball is thrown from the right end in −x at 0.2 m/s relative to the rocket. Find the time, in seconds, when the two balls hit each other.

Accepted answers: 2 s or 8 s

The published question accepts the integer answers 2 or 8. In the rocket frame both balls have the same pseudo-acceleration, so their relative acceleration is zero and the unconstrained relative-motion calculation gives 4/(0.3+0.2)=8 s. The alternative 2 s corresponds to the motion with contact at the chamber end. The earlier page incorrectly claimed 8 s was the only accepted answer.

Q31. Speed of an expanding sphere

JEE Advanced 2017 Paper 2 · PYQ (Code 9 option order)

Consider an expanding sphere of instantaneous radius R whose total mass remains constant. The expansion is such that the instantaneous density ρ remains uniform throughout the volume. The rate of fractional change in density (1/ρ)(dρ/dt) is constant. The velocity v of any point on the surface of the expanding sphere is proportional to:

Answer: C — R

With constant mass, ρ=3M/(4πR³), so (1/ρ)(dρ/dt)=−3v/R. Since the fractional rate is constant, v/R is constant and v∝R. The options and key now match the original 2017 Paper 2 order.

Q32. Two projectiles collide after different launch angles

Relative-motion drill · practice, not PYQ

Two projectiles are launched simultaneously from (0,0) and (L,0). The first is launched at angle θ₀ with speed v₀; the second is launched towards the first at angle 180°−θ₁, with its speed chosen so their vertical velocity components match. The collision times are T₁ for (θ₀,θ₁)=(45°,45°) and T₂ for (θ₀,θ₁)=(60°,30°). Find (T₁/T₂)².

Answer: 2

Equal vertical components require v₁ sinθ₁=v₀ sinθ₀. For 45°/45°, v₁=v₀ and horizontal closing speed is √2v₀, so T₁=L/(√2v₀). For 60°/30°, v₁=√3v₀ and closing speed is v₀/2+(√3v₀)(√3/2)=2v₀, so T₂=L/(2v₀). Thus (T₁/T₂)²=2. This is a newly written exercise, not a historical JEE question.

Q33. Acceleration components on a parabolic trajectory

JEE Advanced 2020 Paper 2 · PYQ

A particle starts from the origin at t=0 with speed 1 m/s and follows the trajectory y=x²/2 (SI units). Its acceleration components are aₓ and aᵧ. Which statements are correct?

Answer: A, B, C and D

From y=x²/2, vᵧ=xvₓ and aᵧ=vₓ²+xaₓ. At the origin vᵧ=0 and the initial speed is 1 m/s, so vₓ=1 and aᵧ=1, proving A. If aₓ=0, vₓ remains 1 and aᵧ=1, proving B. The initial tangent is horizontal, proving C. At t=1 s, x=1 and vᵧ/vₓ=x=1, so the velocity angle is 45°, proving D.

Q34. Average velocity over successive rebounds

JEE Advanced 2019 Paper 2 · PYQ

A ball is projected from the ground at angle θ with speed u₀. After each impact with the ground it rebounds at the same angle with speed reduced to u₀/α, then u₀/α², and so on. The magnitude of average velocity over the entire infinite sequence is 0.8 times the magnitude of average velocity during the first flight. Find α.

Answer: 4

Each flight's horizontal range scales as 1, α⁻², α⁻⁴,…, while its duration scales as 1, α⁻¹, α⁻²,… relative to the first flight. Summing the geometric series gives V̄_total/V̄₁=α/(α+1)=0.8, hence α=4.

Q35. Relative motion of two airplanes

JEE Advanced 2014 Paper 1 · PYQ

Airplanes A and B move at constant velocities in the same vertical plane, at 30° and 60° above the horizontal, respectively. The speed of A is 100√3 m/s. Initially, B is 500 m from A. A sees B moving with a constant velocity perpendicular to A’s line of motion. If A just escapes being hit by B after t₀ seconds, find t₀.

Answer: 5 s

For B's relative velocity to be perpendicular to A's motion, the component of B's velocity along A's direction equals vA. Thus vB cos30°=100√3, giving vB=200 m/s. The perpendicular relative component is vB sin30°=100 m/s, so t₀=500/100=5 s.

Q36. Two projectiles fired from a hill

IIT-JEE 1996 · subjective PYQ

Two guns at the top of a 10 m high hill fire shots at the same speed 5√3 m/s, with a time interval between firings. One shot is fired horizontally and the other at 60° above the horizontal. The shots collide in air. Taking the origin at the foot of the hill directly below the muzzles and g=10 m/s², find (i) the interval between firings in seconds, (ii) the x-coordinate of the collision point in metres, and (iii) its y-coordinate in metres.

Answer: 1 s; 5√3 m (≈8.66 m); 5 m

The 60° shot is fired first. If its elapsed time is T and the horizontal shot is delayed by Δ, equality of horizontal positions gives T=2Δ. Equality of heights gives 7.5T=10TΔ−5Δ². Substituting T=2Δ yields Δ=1 s and T=2 s. Then x=(5√3/2)T=5√3 m and y=10+7.5T−5T²=5 m.
