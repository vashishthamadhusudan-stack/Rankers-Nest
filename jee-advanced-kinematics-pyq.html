Q1. Maximum velocity from an acceleration–time graph

IIT-JEE 2004 · PYQ

A body starts from rest. The acceleration decreases linearly from 10 m/s² at t=0 to zero at t=11 s. Find the maximum velocity attained.

Answer: 55 m/s

Maximum velocity is the area under the positive a–t graph: Δv = ½ × 11 s × 10 m/s² = 55 m/s.

Q2. Is the position–time graph physically possible?

IIT-JEE 1979 · PYQ

The original graph assigns positions A and B to the same instant, as shown schematically. Can it describe one particle's motion?

Answer: D

Position must be single-valued at every instant. A vertical segment in an x–t graph assigns two positions to the same time, so it cannot describe a single particle.

Q3. Velocity–time graph: displacement

Graph drill · practice

A particle's velocity changes linearly from +8 m/s at t=0 to −4 m/s at t=6 s. What is its net displacement in the 6 s interval?

Answer: B (12 m)

The signed area under the v–t graph is displacement. Average velocity for a linear change is (8 + (−4))/2 = 2 m/s, so displacement = 2 × 6 = 12 m.

Q4. Position–time slope

Graph drill · practice

Two straight segments on an x–t graph have slopes +3 m/s and −2 m/s. What do these slopes represent?

Answer: C

The slope of an x–t graph is velocity. Positive slope means positive velocity; negative slope means motion in the negative x direction.

Q5. Velocity–time graph: total distance

Graph drill · practice

Velocity is +4 m/s from t=0 to 3 s and −2 m/s from t=3 to 7 s. Find the total distance travelled.

Answer: 20 m

Distance adds magnitudes of areas: |4|×3 + |−2|×4 = 12+8=20 m. Correct numerical answer is 20 m.

Q6. Acceleration–time graph and velocity

Graph drill · practice

A particle starts from rest. Its acceleration is +2 m/s² for 0–4 s and −1 m/s² for 4–8 s. Select all correct statements.

Answer: A and C

From 0–4 s, Δv=2×4=8 m/s. From 4–8 s, Δv=−1×4=−4 m/s, so final velocity is 4 m/s. It remains positive, so no reversal occurs.

Q7. Projectile trajectory through P

JEE Advanced 2026 · Paper 1

A projectile is launched at 45° and passes through P, which is 5 m horizontally from O and 1 m above O. Find its launch speed in terms of g.

Answer: A

Use y=x tanθ−gx²/(2v²cos²θ). At θ=45°, x=5, y=1: 1=5−25g/v², hence v²=25g/4 and v=5√g/2.

Q8. Average acceleration from perpendicular velocity change

IIT-JEE 1982 · PYQ

A particle's velocity changes from 5 m/s east to 5 m/s north in 10 s. Find the magnitude of average acceleration in m/s² (answer to 3 decimal places).

Answer: 0.707 m/s²

Δv has perpendicular components −5 and +5 m/s, so |Δv|=5√2. Average acceleration magnitude=5√2/10=1/√2≈0.707 m/s².

Q9. Successive-second distances

IIT-JEE 2004 · PYQ

A particle starts from rest and slides down a frictionless incline with constant acceleration. If Sₙ is the distance travelled from t=n−1 s to t=n s, find Sₙ/Sₙ₊₁.

Answer: B

x(t)=½at². Therefore Sₙ=½a[n²−(n−1)²]=½a(2n−1), and Sₙ₊₁=½a(2n+1). Their ratio is (2n−1)/(2n+1).

Q10. Boat crossing in minimum time

IIT-JEE 1983 · PYQ

A river flows from west to east. A swimmer wants to reach the opposite bank in the shortest possible time. Relative to still water, which direction should the swimmer aim?

Answer: A

Crossing time is river width divided by the velocity component perpendicular to the bank. That component is maximized by aiming perpendicular to the bank.

Q11. Two-dimensional harmonic coordinates

IIT-JEE 1999 · PYQ

A particle moves with x=a cos(pt), y=b sin(pt). Select all correct acceleration components.

Answer: A and B

Twice differentiating x and y gives x″=−p²x and y″=−p²y.

Q12. Total distance from a velocity graph

Graph drill · practice

A particle moves with velocity +3 m/s from 0–4 s, then −2 m/s from 4–7 s. Find total distance travelled.

Answer: 18 m

Total distance = |3|×4 + |−2|×3 = 12+6=18 m.

Q13. Velocity–time graph: direction change

Graph drill · practice

Velocity decreases linearly from +6 m/s at t=0 to −6 m/s at t=6 s. At what time does the particle reverse direction?

Answer: C

Velocity changes linearly and symmetrically from +6 to −6 m/s. It crosses zero halfway through the 6 s interval, at t=3 s.

Q14. Projectile after impact

JEE Advanced 2018 · PYQ

A projectile launched at 45° reaches maximum height 120 m. At first impact it loses half its kinetic energy; immediately after rebound its velocity makes 30° with horizontal. Find the next maximum height in metres.

Answer: 30 m

Original maximum height H=u²/(4g)=120, so u²=480g. Half kinetic energy remains, giving rebound speed u/√2. Its vertical component is u/(2√2), so H′=(u²/8)/(2g)=u²/(16g)=30 m.

Q15. Shadow tip speed

JEE Advanced 2023 · PYQ

A person 1.6 m tall walks away from a 4 m lamp post at 60 cm/s. Find the speed of the tip of the shadow relative to the person, in cm/s.

Answer: 40 cm/s

Let x be the person's distance from the post and l the shadow length. Similar triangles give 4/(x+l)=1.6/l, so l=(2/3)x. Hence dl/dt=(2/3)(60)=40 cm/s.

Q16. Speed at the highest point of projectile

IIT-JEE 1984 · PYQ

For a projectile under gravity with no air resistance, which statement is correct at its highest point?

Answer: B

Gravity changes only the vertical velocity. At the top vᵧ=0, while vₓ remains constant; acceleration is still g downward.

Q17. Balls inside an accelerating rocket

JEE Advanced 2014 · PYQ

A rocket accelerates at 2 m/s² in gravity-free space. Its chamber is 4 m long. Balls are thrown simultaneously from opposite ends towards one another at 0.3 m/s and 0.2 m/s relative to the rocket. Find their meeting time in seconds.

Answer: 8 s

In the rocket frame both balls share the same pseudo-acceleration, so their relative acceleration is zero. Closing speed=0.3+0.2=0.5 m/s; time=4/0.5=8 s.

Q18. Projectile enters region with changed gravity

JEE Advanced 2022 · PYQ

A projectile has range d under gravity g. At the highest point it enters a region where effective gravity is g/0.81. Its new total range is d′=nd. Find n.

Answer: 0.95

The first half-range is d/2. In the second region, fall time scales by √(g/g′)=0.9, so the second horizontal segment is 0.9d/2. Thus d′=0.95d.
