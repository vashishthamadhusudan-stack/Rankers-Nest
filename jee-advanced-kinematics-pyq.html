Q1. Maximum velocity from the acceleration–time graph

IIT-JEE 2004 Screening · PYQ

A body starts from rest at time t = 0. The acceleration–time graph is shown. The maximum velocity attained by the body will be:

Answer: B — 55 m/s

Velocity gain is the area under the positive a–t graph: Δv = ½ × 11 × 10 = 55 m/s. Since the body starts from rest, maximum velocity is 55 m/s. The redrawn line and labels were checked against the published 2004 question figure.

Q2. Is the shown time-variation of position physically possible?

IIT-JEE 1979 · subjective PYQ, adapted

Original question (wording lightly normalized): “Answer the following giving reason in brief: Is the time variation of position, shown in the figure observed in nature?”

Answer: D — Not possible

A horizontal line on the original figure corresponds to one time but intersects the curve at two positions A and B. One particle cannot occupy both positions at one instant. This SVG is a redraw checked against the published 1979 figure, not a facsimile.

Q3. Velocity–time graph: displacement

Graph drill · practice

A particle's velocity changes linearly from +8 m/s at t=0 to −4 m/s at t=6 s. What is its net displacement in the 6 s interval?

Answer: B (12 m)

The signed area under the v–t graph is displacement. Average velocity for a linear change is (8 + (−4))/2 = 2 m/s, so displacement = 2 × 6 = 12 m.

Q4. Position–time slope

Graph drill · practice

Two straight segments on an x–t graph have slopes +3 m/s and −2 m/s. What do these slopes represent?

Answer: C

The slope of an x–t graph is velocity. Positive slope means positive velocity; negative slope means motion in the negative x direction.

Q5. Velocity–time graph: total distance

Graph drill · practice

Velocity is +4 m/s from t=0 to 3 s and −2 m/s from t=3 to 7 s. Find the total distance travelled.

Answer: 20 m

Distance adds magnitudes of areas: |4|×3 + |−2|×4 = 12+8=20 m. Correct numerical answer is 20 m.

Q6. Acceleration–time graph and velocity

Graph drill · practice

A particle starts from rest. Its acceleration is +2 m/s² for 0–4 s and −1 m/s² for 4–8 s. Select all correct statements.

Answer: A and C

From 0–4 s, Δv=2×4=8 m/s. From 4–8 s, Δv=−1×4=−4 m/s, so final velocity is 4 m/s. It remains positive, so no reversal occurs.

Q7. Projectile through P — select all correct statements

JEE Advanced 2026 · Paper 1, Q6

A particle is projected from O at angle θ and passes through P, 5 m horizontally away and 1 m above O. Select all correct statements.

Answer: A and B

Trajectory: y=x tanθ−gx²/(2v²cos²θ). At P(5,1), for θ=45°, v²=25g/4 so A is true. The range is v²/g=6.25 m, so the apex is at x=3.125 m, before P; B is true. For θ=30°, solving the trajectory gives an apex before P, so C is false. If θ=tan⁻¹(1/5), the launch line points along OP; substituting into the trajectory gives a different speed than option D, so D is false.

Q8. Average acceleration when velocity turns through 90°

IIT-JEE 1982 · PYQ

A particle is moving eastwards with a velocity of 5 m/s. In 10 s the velocity changes to 5 m/s northwards. The average acceleration in this time is:

Answer: B

Δv = (−5 i + 5 j) m/s, so |Δv| = 5√2 m/s and its direction is north-west. The average acceleration magnitude is |Δv|/10 = 1/√2 m/s².

Q9. Successive-second distances

IIT-JEE 2004 · PYQ

A particle starts from rest and slides down a frictionless incline with constant acceleration. If Sₙ is the distance travelled from t=n−1 s to t=n s, find Sₙ/Sₙ₊₁.

Answer: B

x(t)=½at². Therefore Sₙ=½a[n²−(n−1)²]=½a(2n−1), and Sₙ₊₁=½a(2n+1). Their ratio is (2n−1)/(2n+1).

Q10. River crossing in the shortest time

IIT-JEE 1983 · PYQ

A river is flowing from west to east at a speed of 5 metres per minute. A man on the south bank, capable of swimming at 10 metres per minute in still water, wants to swim across the river in the shortest time. He should swim in a direction:

Answer: A — Due north

Minimum crossing time requires the maximum velocity component perpendicular to the banks. That component is maximum when he swims due north.

Q12. Total distance from a velocity graph

Graph drill · practice

A particle moves with velocity +3 m/s from 0–4 s, then −2 m/s from 4–7 s. Find total distance travelled.

Answer: 18 m

Total distance = |3|×4 + |−2|×3 = 12+6=18 m.

Q13. Velocity–time graph: direction change

Graph drill · practice

Velocity decreases linearly from +6 m/s at t=0 to −6 m/s at t=6 s. At what time does the particle reverse direction?

Answer: C

Velocity changes linearly and symmetrically from +6 to −6 m/s. It crosses zero halfway through the 6 s interval, at t=3 s.

Q14. Projectile after impact

Projectile motion · practice (original-year match pending)

A projectile launched at 45° reaches maximum height 120 m. At first impact it loses half its kinetic energy; immediately after rebound its velocity makes 30° with horizontal. Find the next maximum height in metres.

Answer: 30 m

Original maximum height H=u²/(4g)=120, so u²=480g. Half kinetic energy remains, giving rebound speed u/√2. Its vertical component is u/(2√2), so H′=(u²/8)/(2g)=u²/(16g)=30 m.

Q15. Shadow tip speed

Motion · source verification pending (year match not yet confirmed)

A person 1.6 m tall walks away from a 4 m lamp post at 60 cm/s. Find the speed of the tip of the shadow relative to the person, in cm/s.

Answer: 40 cm/s

Let x be the person's distance from the post and l the shadow length. Similar triangles give 4/(x+l)=1.6/l, so l=(2/3)x. Hence dl/dt=(2/3)(60)=40 cm/s.

Q16. Is the projectile’s speed minimum at the top?

IIT-JEE 1984 · PYQ

True or False: A projectile fired from the ground follows a parabolic path, and its speed is minimum at the top of its path (neglect air resistance).

Answer: A — True

At the highest point, the vertical component of velocity is zero while the horizontal component remains constant. Therefore the speed is minimum there.

Q17. Same launch speed, different masses

IIT-JEE 1983 · PYQ

Two balls of different masses are thrown vertically upward with the same initial speed. Neglecting air resistance, they pass the point of projection during their downward motion with the same speed. Is the statement true or false?

Answer: A — True

With air resistance neglected, both balls have the same acceleration g and conserve mechanical energy. On returning to the launch height, each has the same speed magnitude as its initial speed, independent of mass.

Q18. Projectile enters region with changed gravity

Motion · source verification pending (year match not yet confirmed)

A projectile has range d under gravity g. At the highest point it enters a region where effective gravity is g/0.81. Its new total range is d′=nd. Find n.

Answer: 0.95

The first half-range is d/2. In the second region, fall time scales by √(g/g′)=0.9, so the second horizontal segment is 0.9d/2. Thus d′=0.95d.

Q19. Displacement and work from a time–displacement relation

IIT-JEE 1979 · subjective PYQ

The displacement x of a particle moving in one dimension under a constant force is related to time t by t = √x + 3, where x is in metres and t is in seconds. Find (i) the displacement when velocity is zero, and (ii) the work done by the force in the first 6 seconds.

Answer: (i) 0 m; (ii) 0 J

Using the printed relation, x=(t−3)² and v=2(t−3). Setting v=0 gives t=3 s and x=0. At t=0 and t=6 s, the algebraic velocities are −6 and +6 m/s, so their speed magnitudes are equal. Thus ΔK=½m(vf²−vi²)=0 and net work is zero. Editorial note: the source says “moving in one direction,” although the equation implies a velocity sign change at t=3 s. The answer follows the equation as printed.

Q20. Meeting time for four people chasing around a square

IIT-JEE 1984 · PYQ

Four people start at the corners of a square of side d. Each moves with constant speed v directly towards the next person in cyclic order. Express the meeting time as a multiple of d/v; enter the numerical multiplier.

Answer: 1, so t=d/v

Each person's inward velocity component is v/√2 and the initial corner-to-centre distance is d/√2. Thus meeting time=(d/√2)/(v/√2)=d/v.

Q21. Assertion–Reason: slope of an x–t graph

A–R · practice

Assertion (A): The slope of a position–time graph at an instant gives the instantaneous velocity.

Reason (R): Instantaneous velocity is the limit of average velocity as the time interval approaches zero.

Answer: A

Average velocity is Δx/Δt. In the limit Δt→0, this becomes dx/dt, the tangent slope of the x–t graph; therefore R correctly explains A.

Q22. Assertion–Reason: area under a v–t graph

A–R · practice

Assertion (A): The signed area under a velocity–time graph equals displacement.

Reason (R): Velocity is the time derivative of displacement, so integrating velocity over time gives the change in displacement.

Answer: A

Since v=dx/dt, integrating v over the interval gives Δx. The signed area under the v–t graph therefore equals displacement, so R correctly explains A.

Q23. Maximum speed and distance for a car

IIT-JEE 1978 · PYQ, options added for auto-check

A car accelerates from rest at a constant rate α for some time, after which it decelerates at a constant rate β to come to rest. If the total time lapse is t seconds, evaluate (i) the maximum velocity reached and (ii) the total distance travelled.

Answer: A

Let the acceleration and deceleration times be t1 and t2. Since peak speed is v, t1=v/α and t2=v/β. Thus t=v(1/α+1/β), giving vmax=αβt/(α+β). The v–t graph is a triangle, so s=½vt=αβt²/[2(α+β)].

Q24. Displacement and distance in half a revolution

IIT-JEE 1983 · PYQ, format adapted

A particle moves in a circle of radius R. In half the period of revolution, its displacement magnitude and distance covered are:

Answer: A

After half a revolution, the particle is at the diametrically opposite point. Displacement magnitude is 2R and distance travelled is πR.
