Q1. Assertion–Reason: friction and mechanical energy

JEE Advanced 2007 · Paper 1 · Q10

Assertion: A block moving on a rough horizontal surface slows to rest due to friction. If the same block is projected up the same rough surface inclined at 30° with the same initial speed, the mechanical-energy loss is smaller than on the horizontal surface.
Reason: The coefficient of friction of a surface decreases as its angle of inclination increases.

Choose the correct relation between the statements.

Answer: C

On the horizontal surface, friction dissipates the initial kinetic energy. On the incline, friction acts over a shorter stopping distance because gravity also opposes the upward motion; the official paper marks the assertion true. The coefficient of friction is a property of the contacting surfaces and does not generally decrease merely because the plane is tilted, so the reason is false. Official source: JEE Advanced 2007 Paper 1, Q10.

Q2. Limiting force on a rough 45° incline

JEE Advanced 2011 · Paper 1 · Q41

A block on a rough 45° incline is just prevented from sliding down by a force applied parallel to the plane, directed up the slope. The minimum force needed to push the block up at constant speed is three times this holding force. If (N=10\mu), where \(\mu\) is the coefficient of friction, find \(N\).

Answer: B (N = 5)

For upward motion at limiting friction, F_up = mg(sinθ + μcosθ). To just prevent downward slipping, the applied holding force is F_hold = mg(sinθ − μcosθ). Given F_up = 3F_hold: sinθ + μcosθ = 3sinθ − 3μcosθ, hence 4μcosθ = 2sinθ and μ = tanθ/2 = 1/2. Therefore N = 10μ = 5. Official paper: JEE Advanced 2011 Paper 1, Q41.

Q3. Two touching blocks on an inclined plane

JEE Advanced 2014 · Paper 2 · Q19

Two blocks, \(m_1=1\,\mathrm{kg}\) higher up the slope than \(m_2=2\,\mathrm{kg}\), touch end-to-end along the incline (they are not stacked). The plane contact under \(m_1\) is frictionless; the lower block \(m_2\) has \(\mu_s=\mu_k=0.3\) with the plane. Match each incline angle with the friction acting on \(m_2\).

Angles: P: 5°, Q: 10°, R: 15°, S: 20°.
Possible friction values: 1: \(m_2g\sin\theta\); 2: \((m_1+m_2)g\sin\theta\); 3: \(\mu m_2g\cos\theta\); 4: \(\mu(m_1+m_2)g\cos\theta\).

Answer: D

For small angles, static friction on the lower block can support the downhill component of both blocks, so it is \((m_1+m_2)g\sin\theta\) (P and Q). At 15°, the required friction exceeds the maximum available value for the combined load and the lower block slides; friction is then \(\mu m_2g\cos\theta\) (R). At 20°, the contact between blocks no longer transmits the full combined downhill component and the lower block’s kinetic friction is again \(\mu m_2g\cos\theta\) (S). The official keyed matching is option D. Official paper: JEE Advanced 2014 Paper 2, Q19.

Q4. Ring pushed by a stick while rolling

JEE Advanced 2011 · Paper 1 · Q40

A ring of mass 2 kg and radius 0.5 m is pushed horizontally at its rightmost point by a vertical stick exerting a 2 N force. It rolls without slipping with centre-of-mass acceleration 0.3 m s⁻². Ground friction is sufficient to maintain rolling. If the coefficient of friction between stick and ring is \(P/10\), determine \(P\).

Answer: C (P = 4)

The stick pushes the ring left with normal force 2 N. The ring’s mass is 2 kg and its centre accelerates left at 0.3 m s⁻², so horizontal translation gives ground friction \(f_g=2-ma=2-0.6=1.4\,\mathrm N\) to the right. For a ring, \(I=MR^2=0.5\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}\) and the rolling angular acceleration magnitude is \(\alpha=a/R=0.6\,\mathrm{rad\,s^{-2}}\), so the net torque magnitude is \(I\alpha=0.3\,\mathrm{N\,m}\). Ground friction contributes \(Rf_g=0.7\,\mathrm{N\,m}\); therefore friction from the stick must oppose that torque with magnitude \(f_s=(0.7-0.3)/0.5=0.8\,\mathrm N\). Thus \(\mu=f_s/N=0.8/2=0.4\), giving \(P=4\). Official paper: JEE Advanced 2011 Paper 1, Q40.

Q5. Two blocks and a horizontal pull

Advanced practice · not a PYQ

A 2 kg block rests on a 3 kg block. The floor is smooth and the coefficient of friction between the blocks is 0.25. A horizontal force \(F\) pulls the lower block to the right. Take \(g=10\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\). What is the largest \(F\) for which the upper block does not slip?

Answer: A (12.5 N)

For no slip, the common acceleration is at most \(a_{max}=\mu g=2.5\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\). The total mass is 5 kg, so \(F_{max}=(m_1+m_2)a_{max}=5(2.5)=12.5\,\mathrm N\).

Q6. Atwood machine with an accelerating support

Advanced practice · not a PYQ

A light string passes over a smooth pulley fixed to a support accelerating vertically upward with acceleration \(g/4\). Two masses \(m\) and \(3m\) hang from the ends. Find the magnitude of the acceleration of mass \(3m\) relative to the ground, taking upward as positive for the support.

Answer: C (3g/8 downward)

In the pulley frame, the effective downward acceleration is \(g'=g+g/4=5g/4\). The heavier mass accelerates downward relative to the pulley by \((3m-m)/(3m+m)\,g'=g'/2=5g/8\). Since the pulley itself accelerates upward by \(g/4\), the ground-frame acceleration of the heavier mass is \(5g/8-g/4=3g/8\) downward.

Q7. Apparent weight in a descending lift

Advanced practice · not a PYQ

A student of mass 60 kg stands on a weighing scale in a lift accelerating downward at \(2\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\). Take \(g=10\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\). What does the scale read?

Answer: B (480 N)

Taking downward acceleration as 2 m/s², \(mg-N=ma\), so \(N=m(g-a)=60(10-2)=480\,\mathrm N\).

Q8. Minimum acceleration to prevent slipping on a vertical wall

Advanced practice · not a PYQ

A block is pressed against a vertical wall by a horizontal force. The coefficient of static friction is 0.4. If \(g=10\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\), what is the minimum horizontal force needed to keep a 2 kg block at rest?

Answer: C (50 N)

Maximum static friction is \(f_{max}=\mu N=\mu F\). For equilibrium, \(\mu F\ge mg\), hence \(F_{min}=mg/\mu=20/0.4=50\,\mathrm N\).

Q9. Block on a smooth wedge accelerated horizontally

Advanced practice · not a PYQ

A wedge of angle \(30^\circ\) (surface rises to the right) accelerates to the left on a smooth horizontal floor. A small block remains at rest relative to the wedge. What horizontal acceleration magnitude is required? Take \(g=10\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\).

Answer: A (5.77 m/s²)

In the wedge frame, the pseudo-force acts to the right (opposite the wedge's leftward acceleration). Along the incline, equilibrium requires \(mg\sin\theta=ma\cos\theta\), hence \(a=g\tan\theta=10\tan30^\circ\approx5.77\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\).

Q10. Friction direction between accelerating blocks

Advanced practice · not a PYQ

A 1 kg block sits on a 4 kg block. The floor is smooth. A 10 N horizontal force pulls the upper block to the right; the coefficient of friction between blocks is 0.5 and \(g=10\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\). Which statement is correct?

Answer: B

The friction required for common acceleration would be \(f=m_{lower}F/(m_{upper}+m_{lower})=4(10)/5=8\,\mathrm N\), greater than the maximum static friction \(\mu m_{upper}g=5\,\mathrm N\). They therefore slip. Taking the stated coefficient as kinetic friction for this practice question, friction has magnitude 5 N: the upper block acceleration is \((10-5)/1=5\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\), and the lower block acceleration is \(5/4=1.25\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\).
