JEE Main PYQ · Friction

Q1. During pedalling of a bicycle, the friction exerted by the ground on the front and rear wheels acts respectively:

Answer: A. Backward on front, forward on rear

For a driven rear wheel, the ground’s static friction is forward. The front wheel is pulled by the frame and friction acts backward to provide its rotational acceleration.

JEE Main PYQ · Friction

Q2. A 0.1 kg block is pressed against a vertical wall by a horizontal force of 5 N. If μs = 0.5, find the friction force (g = 9.8 m s⁻²).

Answer: B. 0.98 N

Maximum static friction is 2.5 N, but actual static friction only needs to balance the block’s weight: mg = 0.1×9.8 = 0.98 N.

JEE Main PYQ · Friction

Q3. A horizontal force of 10 N just holds a block stationary against a vertical wall. If μs = 0.2, what is the block’s weight?

Answer: D. 2 N

At limiting equilibrium, friction balances weight and fmax = μN = 0.2×10 = 2 N. Hence weight = 2 N.

Q4. A 2 kg block moving at 6 m s⁻¹ on a horizontal icy surface comes to rest in 10 s. Take g = 10 m s⁻². Find μk.

Answer: C. 0.06

Retardation magnitude = 6/10 = 0.6 m s⁻² = μg. Thus μ = 0.6/10 = 0.06.

JEE Main PYQ · Friction

Q5. Two equal blocks m are connected to a spring of constant k as in the standard friction PYQ; the upper block oscillates with amplitude A without slipping on the lower block. What is the maximum friction force between them?

Answer: A. kA/2

The two-block system has angular frequency √(k/2m). Maximum acceleration is ω²A = kA/(2m). Friction on the lower block is m amax = kA/2.

Q6. A block slides up a rough horizontal surface with initial speed v and stops. If the same block is projected up a 30° rough incline with the same speed, the energy lost to friction is:

Answer: B. Smaller in the incline case

On the incline, gravity shares the work needed to stop the block, so friction dissipates less energy than on the horizontal surface.

JEE Main PYQ · Friction

Q7. A block rests on a rough 30° incline. μs = 0.8 and the friction force is 10 N. Find its mass (g = 10 m s⁻²).

Answer: A. 2.0 kg

For equilibrium, friction balances the downslope component: mg sin30° = 10. Thus 5m = 10, m = 2 kg.

JEE Main PYQ · Friction

Q8. A block released from rest slides distance d down a 45° incline. Its time on the rough incline is n times its time on the smooth incline. Find μk.

Answer: A. 1 − 1/n²

For equal distance from rest, t² ∝ 1/a. On 45°, a_rough = g(sin45°−μcos45°)=g sin45°(1−μ). Therefore t_rough/t_smooth = 1/√(1−μ)=n, so μ=1−1/n².

JEE Main PYQ · Friction

Q9. The upper half of an incline of angle θ is smooth and the lower half is rough. A block released from rest at the top comes to rest exactly at the bottom. Find μk on the lower half.

Answer: C. 2 tanθ

Gravity does work mgL sinθ over the full length. Friction acts over L/2: μmg cosθ(L/2). Equating gives μ=2tanθ.

Q10. A car travelling at 100 m s⁻¹ is stopped by friction on a level road. If μk = 0.5 and g = 10 m s⁻², find stopping distance.

Answer: B. 1000 m

Retardation μg = 5 m s⁻². Using 0 = u²−2as gives s = 100²/(2×5)=1000 m.

JEE Main PYQ · Friction

Q11. A block on an incline of angle θ has coefficient of friction μ. The minimum force parallel to the incline to push it upward is F1; the force needed to just prevent it sliding down is F2. If tanθ = 2μ, find F1/F2.

Answer: D. 3

F1 = mg(sinθ+μcosθ); F2 = mg(sinθ−μcosθ). Their ratio is (tanθ+μ)/(tanθ−μ)=(2μ+μ)/(2μ−μ)=3.

JEE Main PYQ · Friction

Q12. A body moves through a medium where the resisting force is F = −kv². Its mass is 10⁻² kg and initial speed is 10 m s⁻¹. After 10 s its kinetic energy is one-eighth of the initial value. Find k.

Answer: C. 10⁻⁴ kg m⁻¹

Kinetic energy falling to 1/8 means final speed v0/2. From m dv/dt=−kv², integrate between v0 and v0/2 over 10 s to obtain k=10⁻⁴ kg m⁻¹.

JEE Main PYQ · Friction

Q13. A block lies on a surface y=x³/6. The coefficient of friction is 0.5. Find the maximum height at which it can remain without slipping.

Answer: C. 1/6 m

At limiting equilibrium tanθ=μ. The slope dy/dx=x²/2=0.5 gives x=1. Then y=x³/6=1/6 m.

JEE Main PYQ · Friction

Q14. Two blocks of weights 20 N and 100 N are pressed against a wall as in the standard two-block friction problem. The upper block is in equilibrium and friction between the blocks supports it. What friction force must the wall exert on the lower block?

Answer: A. 120 N

The wall must support the lower block’s 100 N weight plus the 20 N friction transferred from the upper block, so the required friction is 120 N.

Q15. A 5 kg hanging mass is connected over a frictionless pulley to a 10 kg block on a horizontal surface. μ = 0.15. What additional mass must be placed on the 10 kg block to make the system just remain at rest? Take g = 10 m s⁻².

Answer: A. 23.3 kg

At impending rest, the hanging weight 50 N is balanced by maximum friction μ(M+m)g. Solve 50=0.15(10+m)10, so 50=1.5(10+m), m=23.3 kg. The corrected option is 23.3 kg.

Q16. A trolley of mass 20 kg on a horizontal surface is connected to a 6 kg hanging mass. The kinetic friction coefficient for the trolley is 0.04. Find the acceleration (g = 10 m s⁻²).

Answer: D. 2 m s⁻²

Friction on trolley = 0.04×20×10 = 8 N. Net driving force = 60−8 = 52 N; total mass = 26 kg. Thus a = 2 m s⁻².

Q17. Which statement about dry friction is correct?

Answer: C. Friction is independent of apparent contact area in the elementary model

In the elementary dry-friction model, friction is approximately independent of apparent contact area. Limiting friction depends on the coefficient and normal reaction.

Q18. A 4 kg block is pulled horizontally by a 20 N force on a rough level surface. If μk = 0.25 and g = 10 m s⁻², find acceleration.

Answer: B. 2.5 m s⁻²

Friction = μmg = 0.25×4×10=10 N. Net force =10 N, so acceleration =10/4=2.5 m s⁻².

Q19. A block of mass m rests on a rough horizontal surface. A horizontal force F is applied, but the block does not move. The friction force is:

Answer: C. Equal to F up to its limiting value

Static friction adjusts to equal the applied force while the block remains at rest, up to fmax=μsN.

Q20. A block slides down an incline of angle 30° with constant speed. Find μk.

Answer: A. 1/√3

Constant speed means zero net force along the incline: mg sin30°=μmg cos30°. Thus μ=tan30°=1/√3.

Q21. A 10 kg block slides on a horizontal surface with μk=0.2. What is the magnitude of friction (g=10 m s⁻²)?

Answer: C. 20 N

Kinetic friction = μkN = μkmg = 0.2×10×10=20 N.

Q22. A block is just about to slide down an incline of angle θ. What is the relation between μs and θ?

Answer: C. μs=tanθ

At limiting equilibrium, mg sinθ = μs mg cosθ, so μs=tanθ.
