1. Quick theory notes

  • Gas pressure results from molecular collisions with container walls.
  • For an ideal gas, absolute temperature measures average translational kinetic energy per molecule.
  • At the same temperature, all ideal gases have the same average translational kinetic energy per molecule, regardless of molecular mass.
Pressure relation: P = (1/3) ρ crms2
RMS speed: crms = √(3RT/M) = √(3kT/m)
Mean translational kinetic energy: ⟨K⟩ = (3/2) kT per molecule

ρ is gas density, R is the gas constant, M is molar mass, k is Boltzmann's constant and m is molecular mass.

2. Worked example

Question: If absolute temperature increases from T to 4T, what happens to RMS speed?

Solution: Since crms ∝ √T, the speed ratio is √(4T/T) = 2. RMS speed doubles.

4. Answer key & explanations

Reveal solutions
1. B — (3/2)kT. Three translational degrees of freedom each contribute (1/2)kT.
2. C — Molecular or molar mass. crms = √(3RT/M), so at fixed T a lighter gas has greater RMS speed.
3. B — 3 times. RMS speed is proportional to the square root of absolute temperature: √9 = 3.
4. C — P = (1/3)ρcrms². This is the standard pressure relation from kinetic theory.

5. 30-second recap

  • ⟨K⟩ = (3/2)kT
  • crms ∝ √(T/M)
  • P = (1/3)ρcrms²
